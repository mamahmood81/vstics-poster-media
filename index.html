vSTICS Poster Visualizations

1. Flow Simulation

Figure 1. vSTICS simulation, correlation analysis, and flow extraction. Simulated intensity volumes were generated using a Gaussian PSF with widths σx = 1.5 px, σy = 1.5 px, and σz = 7.5 px, a particle density of 0.0024 particles/pixel³, and a 128 × 128 × 128 pixel volume. A uniform flow of 1 µm/s was seeded along the x-direction.

(A) Simulated Intensity Volume

The intensity data are displayed as a maximum-intensity projection of the simulated 3D volume.

(B) Spatiotemporal Autocorrelation Function

The volumetric autocorrelation function was calculated using 64 × 64 × 64 pixel ROIs with a spatial shift of 4 pixels and TOI windows of 8 frames shifted by 2 frames. The correlation volume was cropped around the origin and fit with an asymmetric 3D Gaussian function.

(C) Flow Extraction

Flow was extracted from the fitted Gaussian peak displacements. Peak movement across time was fit with a linear function, and the resulting velocity field was visualized as a color-coded quiver plot.

Recovered Density Map

The local particle density was recovered from the temporal autocorrelation amplitude and rendered spatially across the simulated volume.

2. Diffusion and Flow Simulation

Figures 2 and 3. vSTICS simulation, diffusion extraction, and density recovery. Simulated intensity volumes were generated using a Gaussian PSF with widths σx = 1.5 px, σy = 1.5 px, and σz = 7.5 px, a particle density of 0.0024 particles/pixel³, and a 128 × 128 × 128 pixel volume. Particles diffused with D = 0.001 µm²/s.

(A) Simulated Intensity Volume

The simulated 3D intensity data are displayed as a maximum-intensity projection.

(B) Spatiotemporal Autocorrelation Function Views

The volumetric autocorrelation function was calculated using 64 × 64 × 64 pixel ROIs with a spatial shift of 4 pixels and TOI windows of 8 frames shifted by 2 frames. The correlation peak was fit with an asymmetric 3D Gaussian model.

(C) Flow Extraction

The fitted Gaussian peak displacements were used to recover the local velocity field, visualized as a color-coded quiver plot.

(D) Density Recovery

Particle density was recovered from the zero-lag temporal autocorrelation amplitude, normalized by the ROI volume and effective PSF volume, then rendered as a 3D spatial density distribution.

(E) Diffusion Recovery

Local diffusion coefficients were extracted from the temporal autocorrelation decay and visualized as a volumetric rendering of recovered D values.

3. MitoTracker Green Mitochondrial Labelling

Figure 5. 3D mapping of mitochondrial transport dynamics in a growing Camellia japonica pollen tube. Mitochondria were labelled with MitoTracker Green FM and analyzed by vSTICS. Sampling used 64 × 64 pixel windows in x-y and 16 pixels in z, shifted by 5 pixels laterally and 2 pixels axially, with a TOI of 4 frames shifted by 1 frame.

(A) Mitochondrial Fluorescence Rendering

Volumetric rendering of mitochondrial fluorescence at the pollen tube tip.

(B) 3D Velocity Field

Corresponding 3D velocity field displayed as a color-coded quiver plot overlaid on the reconstructed pollen tube geometry, revealing mitochondrial motion relative to the pollen tube growth axis.

4. FM1-43 Vesicle Labelling

Figures 6 and 7. Vesicle organization and vSTICS analysis of FM1-43-labelled vesicle dynamics. A growing Camellia japonica pollen tube labelled with FM1-43 was imaged over a 55 × 55 × 10 µm³ volume at 0.5 s per volume. vSTICS analysis used the same sampling parameters as the mitochondrial dataset.

(A) FM1-43 Intensity Rendering

Volumetric rendering of FM1-43 fluorescence in the pollen tube apex, showing vesicle-associated fluorescence within the apical region.

(B) 3D Velocity Field

Three-dimensional velocity vector field obtained from vSTICS analysis of the FM1-43 signal, displayed as a color-coded quiver plot over the reconstructed apical volume.

(C) Density Rendering

Local particle densities were recovered from the zero-lag temporal autocorrelation amplitude and rendered across the pollen tube apex.
